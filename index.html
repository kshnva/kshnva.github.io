About

I am a computational scientist with a background in mechanical engineering, currently completing an MSc in Computational Science at the University of Amsterdam. My work sits at the boundary between physics-based modelling and data-driven methods: I am most interested in problems where a simulation has to be built, calibrated against real measurements, and then interrogated at scale.

That has taken me across a deliberately wide range of systems: alloy thermodynamics from DFT-fitted models and Monte Carlo, granular force networks with discrete-element simulation, blood flow in the cerebral microvasculature reconstructed from electron-microscopy volumes, cascading failures on power grids, epidemics on networks, crowd dynamics with agent-based models, stochastic pricing and calibration in finance, and parallel search on a 256-core HPC cluster.

What connects these projects is the approach rather than the domain: write down the physics or the dynamics, choose a numerical method that respects it, validate against data, and then use the model to ask questions that experiments alone cannot. I am looking for a PhD where I can keep doing that at depth, in a group that values both rigorous methods and well-engineered, reproducible code.

Research interests

  • Numerical methods for PDEs

    Finite-difference and finite-volume solvers, iterative methods (Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, SOR), eigenvalue problems, time integration and stability.

  • Atomistic & mesoscale simulation

    Cluster expansion, Monte Carlo (canonical & semi-grand canonical), effective-medium potentials, alloy thermodynamics and phase separation.

  • Particle & network models

    Discrete-element simulation of granular media, force-network analysis, flow on vascular and power-grid networks, agent-based models.

  • Simulation-driven machine learning

    Surrogate models, Gaussian-process regression, genetic algorithms and CMA-ES for design-space exploration and inverse problems.

  • High-performance & scientific computing

    Distributed parallelism in Julia and Python, strong-scaling analysis, reproducible simulation pipelines.

  • Stochastic modelling

    Stochastic differential equations, Monte Carlo variance reduction, calibration of stochastic models to data.
